Browser-use visuals

How the assistant's work in the browser reads at a glance. Built on the tokens and components of browser-ui.html. Every state is shown with an icon shape and a word, never colour alone, and nothing needs motion to be understood. New items are marked NEW; changed existing components are listed in the hand-off note.

The one-second answer. A person looks at one of three places and gets state, place, action and progress: the run pill on the page card edge, the run bar above the composer, or the sidebar run chip. The rule that decides which one speaks: the page is framed only when the current step touches that page; tab and Space changes speak in the sidebar; changes to the app's own store (artifacts, skills, Spaces) speak in the chat and the sidebar row they create.

1 · Status language NEW

Nine states, used the same way in the chat, on the page and in the sidebar. Waiting for you is always amber, never moves, and always says what it is waiting for. Blocked by a safety rule is not an error: it is red with a shield and carries the reason in plain words.

2 · Run bar (chat) and run pill (page) NEW

The run bar sits above the composer, in the compact bar and in the expanded chat. The pill hangs from the top edge of the page card, drawn by the app above the page. Both carry: state word, verb and target, step position, Stop. Step position is a row of ticks (done, current, to do) so the count reads without numbers; when the plan is not known it reads "Step 4 · up to 25".

3 · The step list

Changed: the ToolCallRow gains a site chip, a result line and group rows. Raw tool names and arguments move behind a quiet "Details" that appears on hover or keyboard focus (and in the row's menu). Groups collapse repeated reads into one row with the sites' icons; the group's status is the worst unfinished child.

4 · The approval card

Changed: ApprovalCard now shows where (site chip), what (target in plain words, with a picture of the element where there is one), what goes in (exact typed text or cleaned link), what happens next, the risk, and what happens if the person does nothing. Keys are suggested: Enter allows, Shift+Enter allows the rest of the reply, Esc does not allow; risky cards need Ctrl+Enter and have no "rest".

5 · On the page

Everything here is drawn by the app in its own layer above the web page, so the page cannot fake it, hide it or read it. The edge frame says "the assistant is in this page"; the marker says "this exact element"; both go away the moment the step ends. After a click or type, a quiet outlined label stays on the element ("Clicked", "Typed") until the next step starts. Titles and sites in the pill are cleaned (no query strings).

6 · Sidebar and strip

Changed: TabRow and SpaceGroup. The existing blue working dot is kept and gets three siblings: amber (waiting), red (stuck), hollow (will be changed by a batch waiting for approval). Tabs the assistant opened carry a small sparkle that explains itself on hover. The collapsed strip shows the same dot on the favicon and a count on the Space icon.

6b · Tab management: five ways to mark tabs a batch will change

The dotted outlines are gone. Same state in every column: six tabs will move (three to Shopping, three to Research), three will close, one is untouched, and the approval card is waiting. A is the one used in the scenarios now (recommended). All five clear on approve, decline or Stop, and all pair a word with the colour.

7 · View modes

Page only: the pill on the page edge is the whole interface; hovering or focusing it opens the last three steps, and approvals appear as a popup-layer card on the page. Chat only: the run bar names the page being worked on (cleaned title, site, Space) with a small screenshot on hover, because the page itself is not on screen. Full frames for both are scenarios 7 and 8 below.

9 · Run-level moments

Starting, waiting on a page, waking a sleeping tab, a tab closed by the person, a limit, Stop, finishing, and finishing while the person is elsewhere. The finished and stopped pills stay until dismissed (click, Esc, or the next prompt): nothing disappears on a timer, and an old frame can never look like current work.
